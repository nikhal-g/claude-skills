# Offer Idea Generator

The offer is the reason someone should care. Everything else — the subject line, the research, the copy — is just a delivery mechanism for the offer. If the offer is weak, no amount of craft in the email will save it.

This skill generates specific, testable offer ideas tailored to your ICP.

---

## When to Use

- When creating a new cold email campaign
- When refreshing an underperforming campaign (usually the offer is the problem, not the copy)
- When someone says "what should I offer?", "generate angles", "brainstorm hooks", "why would they reply"
- When ICP onboarding is complete and you need to decide what to put in the email
- After `/client-intake` when the ICP is defined but the offer isn't nailed down

---

## The Offer Stack

Not every offer works for every ICP. There are five types of offers, ranked by how much friction they require:

| Type | Friction | When to Use |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Insight** | None | First email — lowest barrier |
| **Framework** | None | When you have expertise they're seeking |
| **Audit/Assessment** | Low | When you can spot gaps quickly |
| **Template/Tool** | Medium | When your tool solves their problem |
| **Call/Demo** | High | Only when they're already warm |

**Rule:** Start with Insight or Framework for the first email. Save Audit/Template for follow-ups. Never lead with a Call/Demo unless you're replying to warm traffic.

---

## The Five Offer Categories

Use these as a menu. Most campaigns should lead with one primary offer and have a backup offer for follow-ups.

---

### 1. The Specific Observation (Insight)

**What it is:** A finding about their industry, company, or role that suggests a problem or opportunity.

**Why it works:** Zero friction — they don't have to do anything. Just reading it adds value.

**Formula:** "[Specific fact about them] + [implication]"

**Examples:**
- "Most SaaS companies in your stage spend 40% more on onboarding than necessary because of how they structure their trial flow — I see this with teams under 50 people"
- "Your LinkedIn engagement dropped 30% in Q3 even though posting frequency stayed the same — usually means the algorithm is deprioritizing your content type"
- "Companies adding a second fulfillment option see 15-25% reduction in cart abandonment, but most add it wrong and still lose half the recovered carts"

**When to use:** First email in any sequence, any ICP.

---

### 2. The Mini Framework (Framework)

**What it is:** A repeatable method or model that helps them solve a problem — simplified and shareable.

**Why it works:** Frameworks feel like intellectual property. They want to apply it. They're also easy to reference in follow-ups.

**Formula:** "[X-step process] for [specific outcome]"

**Examples:**
- "The three-question filter we use to cut unqualified vendor demos by 60%"
- "The four-part audit we run on cold email sequences before they go live"
- "The lead scoring matrix that doubled our sales team's time-on-prospects"

**When to use:** When you have a repeatable methodology and the ICP is looking to learn or improve.

---

### 3. The Gap Audit (Audit/Assessment)

**What it is:** A mini-audit that reveals a gap in their current approach — delivered in the email itself.

**Why it works:** People want to know what they're missing. If you can identify a gap in the email, they want to know more.

**Formula:** "[Do this] and you'll find [specific gap]"

**Examples:**
- "Run your current unsubscribe rate against industry benchmarks — most companies in your space are 2-3x higher than they think"
- "Pull your top 50 replies and categorize them — I'll bet you find that 40% are asking the same question you've already answered in your copy"
- "Check your average time-to-reply for warm inbound vs cold outbound — if there's more than a 4-hour gap, your response handling is costing you pipeline"

**When to use:** When you can spot a gap quickly from public data or industry benchmarks.

---

### 4. The Ready-Made Template (Template/Tool)

**What it is:** A specific template, tool, or resource they can use immediately.

**Why it works:** Tangible value. They can use it today. Low risk.

**Formula:** "Our [name] that [specific outcome] — happy to send it over"

**Examples:**
- "The email sequence template we use for product launches — 6 emails that averaged 18% reply rate in our tests"
- "The spreadsheet our clients use to track warm leads — includes the reply classification guide we built internally"
- "The outreach script we wrote for our last client in your space — they've used it for 6 months without changing a word"

**When to use:** When you have proven assets from past work or have built something specifically for their situation.

---

### 5. The Expert Call (Call/Demo)

**What it is:** A 15-30 minute call to discuss their specific situation.

**Why it works:** Maximum signal. Only interested people accept.

**Why it fails:** High friction for a cold contact. Should never be the first ask.

**Formula:** "Quick call to [specific outcome] if [condition is true]"

**Examples:**
- "Worth a quick call if you're currently spending more than $X/month on [problem]"
- "Happy to jump on a call if you're looking at [market/technology/category] this quarter"
- "If the sizing issue is actually costing you, a 15-minute call to talk through what we've seen with similar teams would probably save you a few iterations"

**When to use:** Second or third email follow-up. After they've shown interest. Never the first ask.

## The Offer Selection Matrix

Use this to pick the right offer for your ICP:

| Their Situation | Best Offer |
|-----------------|------------|
| Unaware of a problem they have | Observation (make them aware) |
| Aware but don't know how to solve | Framework (show the method) |
| Know the problem, don't know their gap | Gap Audit (reveal the gap) |
| Know the gap, need the tool | Template/Tool |
| Already convinced, just needs a push | Call |

**Most cold email starts with Situation 1-3.** If your first email is asking for a call and they're in Situation 1, you're dead on arrival.

## Generating Offer Ideas: The Worksheet

When generating offers for a specific campaign, answer these questions:

**About your ICP:**
1. What problem are they hired to solve?
2. What keeps them up at night related to this problem?
3. What have they already tried?
4. What would a success look like in 90 days?
5. Who else touches this problem besides them?

**About your solution:**
1. What's something counterintuitive about your approach?
2. What's a common mistake you see in their situation?
3. What data or insight do you have that they probably don't?
4. What would you charge $X for if you offered it as a standalone product?
5. What's the simplest version of what you do?

**About the offer:**
1. What's free, specific, and immediately useful?
2. What's a question they ask in every first call?
3. What's one thing that would make them say "how do you know that about us"?
4. What's a pattern you've seen across 10+ clients in their situation?
5. What would be valuable even if they never become a customer?

## The Offer Test

Before you commit to an offer, run it through this test:

**The Reply Test:**
> "Would [specific person in your ICP] reply to an email with this offer?"

- If no: The offer is wrong or the targeting is wrong
- If maybe: Sharpen the specificity
- If yes: Test it

**The Replace Test:**
> "Could this offer be in any cold email from any vendor?"

- If yes: It's too generic — add your unique angle
- If no: It's specific enough to stand out

**The One-Week Test:**
> "If they reply asking for this offer, can I deliver it in one week?"

- If no: The offer is too complex — simplify
- If yes: You're ready

## Common Offer Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| "Book a demo" as first ask | Too much commitment for cold | Move to Insight/Framework first |
| Generic value prop | "We help companies X" | Add specific outcome with context |
| Vague offer | "Learn more" | Make it specific: "The X we built for Y" |
| Me-focused | "Our company does X" | Make it them-focused: "What I see in [their situation]" |
| No follow-up offer | First email has to do all the work | Plan 2-3 follow-ups with different offers |
